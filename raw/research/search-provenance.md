## Scope and sequence

The original thesis ingest was completed and committed as 8d431fa before the continuation research began. The search covered work after the 2014 thesis through 2026-11-19. Related older concepts in the thesis were treated as historical context, not revalidated by assumption. This was a targeted narrative search, not a systematic review or an exhaustive novelty assessment.

Separate search angles covered direct PpIX/ascorbate and related-group continuation; albumin/speciation and opposing effects; product identification; clinical PpIX/PDT milestones and activation methods; and adjacent intravenous-ascorbate clinical trials. Compilation was performed sequentially by the primary researcher after parallel source collection.

## Search outcomes and limits

- Related Bagdonas-group work was located in a 2020 PpIX/ascorbate EPR conference abstract, a 2022 hematoporphyrin-derivative journal study and a 2026 HMME/pH conference abstract. No source found explicitly establishes continuation from the supplied Vargonas thesis, and no subsequent Vargonas-authored paper was identified in this bounded search.
- No independent structural identification of the exact thesis-associated bacteriochlorin-like product, or a clinical trial validating the exact PpIX/ascorbate/BSA combination, was located. These are bounded search outcomes, not claims of worldwide absence.
- Negative evidence was deliberately selected: ascorbate protection against ALA-PDT in a cell study, context-dependent albumin protection, failed overall clinical ascorbate endpoints, and a PDT active-comparator trial with inferior durability.
- A 2026 primary PpIX LC-MS/MS study and the FDA's 2026 ALA-PDT sBCC approval were included as particularly relevant recent advances. Their experimental and indication limits were preserved.
- Nanomaterial, hypoxia, immunotherapy and other photosensitizer literature is much larger than the selected evidence set. Broad platform reviews, redundant older indications and unverified commercial claims were excluded. The focused selection does not rank every new therapy in oncology.

## Access and extraction

- Intermittent PMC reCAPTCHA was bypassed by reading original full-text XML from Europe PMC. This succeeded for the selected clinical and albumin papers; no cached summary was substituted.
- A mechanistic iron/ascorbate author-manuscript XML request returned an error and was not used as a source. The selected ALA/iron-regulation nanomedicine paper was read through its accessible original XML.
- The main-text tables in the clinical narrative XML extracts lost cell separators. The original snapshots were retained; separate table supplements preserve rows, cell boundaries and footnotes. Scientific conclusions were checked against the original paper text and table supplements.
- The Jansen publisher page returned 403; the author-institution original PDF was accessed instead. Its numerical facts were transcribed with source-page locators.
- Sanai's complete PubMed abstract was accessible, but publisher full text and supplementary information were not verified. Only abstract-level conclusions were used. The recruiting phase 2 trial was separately verified from current native registry fields.
- The original FDA letters, FDA clinical review, manufacturer label and PMDA English review were inspected. The FDA decision date, rather than the later sponsor press-release date, was used for the recent AMELUZ milestone.
- The University of Osaka version-of-record PDF was accessed with normal-validation PowerShell networking after a Python certificate-trust failure. It was not replaced by a review's summary.
- License and coverage metadata accompany each source. Open-licensed source text is retained with attribution and applicable noncommercial restrictions. Where no reuse license was verified, short original excerpts are accompanied, when necessary, by transcribed factual data using editorial field names. Analyst interpretation is compiled in wiki articles, not presented as an original-source quotation.
- Conference abstracts are explicitly distinguished from full journal studies; original-document figures were not numerically digitized. No experiments or clinical interventions were performed.
