# Limitations and original continuation questions

> Sources: Danielius Vargonas, 2014, thesis methods, discussion and conclusions; defense presentations
> Raw: [Thesis text](../../raw/thesis/thesis-text.md); [PDF presentation](../../raw/thesis/presentation-pdf-text.md); [PPTX presentation](../../raw/thesis/presentation-pptx-text.md)
> Updated: 2026-11-19

## What the original author proposed next

The defense presentations propose lowering PpIX concentration by orders of magnitude and using additional solvents to reduce the aggregation that prevented clear acidic-condition measurements. The editable presentation additionally proposes checking the Fenton reaction mechanism.

Those suggestions address the actual unresolved experiment: how to observe and identify the chemistry in acidic model solutions. They do not establish a therapeutic protocol.

## Questions left open by the evidence

| Question | Why it remains open |
|----------|---------------------|
| What molecules form? | Product identity was inferred from overlapping absorption bands; no isolation or structural confirmation is supplied. |
| Does BSA act as a proton acceptor? | That explanation was inferred from parallel spectral patterns; binding and aggregation were not independently controlled. |
| Does ascorbate improve useful photosensitization? | Product formation and bleaching were measured, without a direct reactive-species yield or biological efficacy endpoint. |
| Does the mechanism persist under low oxygen? | The motivation and diagrams invoke hypoxia, but the main pH study supplies no direct oxygen measurement or controlled oxygen series. |
| Is Fenton chemistry involved? | No iron-control, iron-speciation or radical-mechanism experiment is reported; the presentation explicitly leaves this for further work. |
| Is the pH effect independent of formulation? | PpIX/BSA concentrations and buffer composition vary between conditions, and final DMSO content is unspecified. |
| Are differences reproducible? | Independent replicates and uncertainty are not reported in the extracted prose. No separate instrument-data archive was supplied; embedded graph objects were not reverse-engineered into numerical data. |

## Historical context needs separate verification

The thesis literature review contains broad statements about cancer treatment, tissue acidity and porphyria. In particular, its historical explanation involving vampirism and drinking blood has no demonstrated support in the supplied work and is not adopted as wiki knowledge. More generally, the old literature-review narrative is not a current clinical evidence review.

Any continuation must distinguish the author's findings from later work, distinguish direct PpIX/ascorbate evidence from adjacent photosensitizers or standalone intravenous ascorbate, and report negative or contradictory studies alongside positive results. This article records the baseline questions; later research should link back to them without rewriting what the original experiment established.
