## Observations by environment

| Environment | What the thesis reports | Interpretation and boundary |
|-------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Acidic, pH=5 | Strong aggregation, some visible aggregates, noisy changes and no clear new product bands | Aggregation and possible sedimentation obscured the photochemistry. This does not establish that photochemical reactions were absent. |
| Neutral, pH=7 | PpIX bleaching; spectral shifts after adding BSA; distinct new features in ascorbate-containing mixtures | Albumin changes the molecular environment and apparent aggregation state. New absorption features support product formation, without proving structure or therapeutic value. |
| Alkaline, pH=11 | Fewer large aggregates; bands assigned predominantly to dimers; product features with ascorbate even without BSA | The dependence on BSA differs from neutral solution. This is a mechanistic comparison in strongly alkaline solution, not a physiological treatment condition. |

## Photoproduct assignments

The manuscript associates a feature at 662 nm with formyl-group porphyrin products, a feature at 670 nm in the neutral PpIX/ascorbate mixture with a chlorin-like product, and paired features around 410-420nm and 660-670nm with a bacteriochlorin-like substance. The slides emphasize 420 nm and 672 nm.

These descriptions are retained as the author's assignments. The discussion itself acknowledges overlapping bands and shifted apparent maxima. Absorption peaks alone do not distinguish a chemically pure product from overlapping products, altered aggregation or binding-dependent spectral shifts. The supplied work includes no isolated-product structural identification. Even the alkaline aggregation assignment has a transcription or interpretation mismatch: the results prose gives a shift to 384 nm, while the final conclusion names 377/396 nm. Neither value has been silently substituted for the other.

The strongest bacteriochlorin-like signature was assigned to the combined PpIX/BSA/ascorbate sample at neutral pH, and to the ascorbate-containing samples at alkaline pH. The neutral mixture without BSA was instead discussed as chlorin-like. Treating every ascorbate-containing product as the same molecule would lose this distinction.

## Albumin: observation versus mechanism

The thesis interprets BSA-related spectral changes as PpIX monomerization and enhanced photobleaching, with some increase in product formation. At neutral pH, BSA and ascorbate together favored the proposed bacteriochlorin-like signature. At alkaline pH, adding BSA no longer produced the same enhancement; the slides use stronger suppression language than the manuscript's emphasis on competition.

The author proposes that BSA acts as a proton acceptor at neutral pH because lowering proton availability in alkaline solution produces a related spectral pattern without BSA. This is a mechanistic hypothesis. The experiments do not isolate proton transfer from changes in protein conformation, binding, aggregation, buffer composition or starting concentration.

## Hypoxia and Fenton chemistry

The tissue model includes low oxygen and reduction of ferric to ferrous iron. These motivate a possible ascorbate-assisted pathway. However, the main pH study does not report direct oxygen measurements, iron-speciation measurements, radical detection or a controlled iron-addition experiment. The presentation's suggestion to test Fenton chemistry is evidence that this remained future work.

The PDF also refers to hypoxia obtained in earlier laser coursework. That statement must not be transferred to every LED/pH experiment as a verified oxygen condition.

## Version discrepancy at acidic pH

The legacy PPT states that acidic photoprocesses did not occur, whereas the PPTX states that they were not observed. The manuscript's aggregation and detection limitations support the narrower non-observation statement. This discrepancy is preserved rather than resolved by treating all presentation formats as interchangeable.
