## Most directly relevant therapeutic milestone

**The FDA approved AMELUZ's superficial basal cell carcinoma indication on 2026-09-09.** This expands an ALA-based treatment that generates PpIX and uses red-light PDT. The approved adult indication and specified lamp combination matter: this is an indication-specific clinical advance, not approval of arbitrary PpIX preparations or ascorbate combinations.

The label's pivotal sBCC study treated 187 subjects, with 145 receiving AMELUZ and 42 vehicle. Main-target-lesion complete response combining clinical and histological assessment was 66% versus 5%, assessed 12 weeks after the start of the last PDT cycle. Clinical-only main-target response was 83% versus 21%; those higher figures should not be described as confirmed tumor eradication. A separate subject-level definition required clinical clearing of all target lesions, main and additional: residual sBCC was found histologically in 26/119 (22%) of those AMELUZ responders. The main-target and all-target response denominators differ.

Application-site pain occurred in 95% of AMELUZ recipients. The study population was entirely White, without Fitzpatrick skin types V or VI. These are material limits on interpretation and generalization. The trial was vehicle-controlled and does not establish superiority to surgery, long-term cure or a survival advantage.

## Other established milestones need precise labels

| Milestone | What changed | Relation to this project |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------------|
| FDA Gleolan approval, 2017-06-06 | ALA-induced PpIX fluorescence became an approved adjunct for visualizing malignant tissue during glioma surgery in the United States | A direct PpIX clinical application, but surgical guidance rather than tumor-destructive PDT. |
| TOOKAD EU authorization, 10 November 2017 | Padeliporfin vascular-targeted PDT for selected men with unilateral low-risk prostate cancer | Shows clinical translation of a different photosensitizer and vascular target; does not validate the thesis chemistry. |
| Akalux, Japan, September 2020 | EGFR-targeted near-infrared photoimmunotherapy for unresectable locally advanced or recurrent head and neck cancer | An adjacent targeted activation strategy using an antibody/IR700 conjugate, distinct from PpIX and ascorbate. |

The FDA's Gleolan assessment found supportive evidence for greater completeness of resection but no persuasive survival or progression-free-survival advantage in the cited randomized study. Its low negative predictive value means that absence of fluorescence does not exclude tumor. These are limits of the reviewed evidence, not a claim that every later surgical outcome study is negative.

The PMDA Akalux authorization assessment relied on limited clinical data and included postmarketing surveillance and further efficacy/safety conditions. An objective response in a small uncontrolled cohort is not an established survival gain. Japan-specific approval must not be generalized to other jurisdictions.

## PDT is not automatically the best comparator

Jansen and colleagues' randomized study of field treatment for actinic keratoses is an important counterweight to approval milestones. It randomized 624 patients and analyzed 602 in the modified intention-to-treat population. At 12 months, the cumulative probability of remaining free from treatment failure was 74.7% for fluorouracil and 37.7% for MAL-PDT under the studied protocols.

The endpoint required at least 75% lesion-count reduction; it was not complete removal of every lesion. This was a particular MAL-PDT protocol, rather than a comparison of every ALA formulation or the new sBCC indication. It shows why durability and a relevant active comparator matter when claiming improvement. Historical comparator arms are not current treatment recommendations.

## PpIX sonodynamic therapy: a frontier, not established efficacy

Sanai and colleagues reported an early human study in nine patients with recurrent high-grade glioma using an ALA precursor and focused ultrasound. The abstract reports dose-related changes in oxidative-stress and cell-death markers between treated and untreated tumor regions, and no drug-related or device-related toxicities. It does not provide marker effect sizes. The source coverage here is the abstract and bibliographic record; publisher full text and supplements were not verified.

This provides early human pharmacodynamic and feasibility evidence, not a randomized survival result. Ultrasound activation also differs from optical PDT and does not establish oxygen independence or benefit from vitamin C.

The current NCT07225621 registry snapshot describes a recruiting randomized, masked phase 2 study with estimated enrollment of 103. Its latest posted update is 2026-07-17, and no results are posted. Estimated enrollment is not the number already treated. This indicates movement toward controlled efficacy testing; it does not supply the answer. Recruitment status is a point-in-time registry fact.
