# Intravenous ascorbate: clinical signals and failures

## Why this belongs beside the thesis

The thesis proposed using ascorbate's context-dependent redox chemistry to improve photosensitization. Later clinical research has tested pharmacological intravenous ascorbate with chemotherapy. That is an adjacent translational development: these trials did not use PpIX or light and do not validate a PpIX/ascorbate PDT combination. Their differing results make a general claim that vitamin C improves cancer therapy untenable.

## A meaningful positive signal in pancreatic cancer

Bodeker and colleagues' randomized phase 2 study compared gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel with or without pharmacological ascorbate in metastatic pancreatic cancer. It randomized thirty-six participants and analyzed 34 who received assigned treatment. Median overall survival was 16 months with ascorbate versus 8.3 months in the control group; the reported hazard ratio was 0.46, with a 90% confidence interval of 0.23 to 0.92. Median progression-free survival was 6.2 versus 3.9 months.

**Significance:** a randomized survival signal is a substantial advance over solution spectra, animal experiments or uncontrolled case series. **Limit:** this was a small study stopped at interim analysis after a strong efficacy signal. It used a treated/evaluable cohort, reported 90% intervals, and the authors identify limited sample size and diversity. The effect size is a reason for confirmatory testing, rather than an established broadly applicable treatment effect. The quality-of-life analyses were exploratory and did not show a significant global-health difference; they should not be headlined as a proven quality-of-life improvement.

## A larger trial did not show overall benefit

The phase 3 VITALITY study randomized 442 patients with previously untreated metastatic colorectal cancer. Adding high-dose IV vitamin C to FOLFOX with or without bevacizumab did not meet the primary progression-free-survival endpoint: median PFS was 8.6 versus 8.3 months, with HR 0.86 and P = 0.1. Overall survival and response rate were also not significantly improved in the overall population.

A prespecified RAS-mutant subgroup had longer PFS, 9.2 versus 7.8 months. That supports biomarker-focused further research, while leaving the overall negative primary result intact. The trial cannot be used as proof of benefit for all colorectal cancers or for unrelated photosensitization protocols.

## A placebo-controlled prostate trial stopped for futility

Paller and colleagues compared docetaxel plus IV vitamin C with docetaxel plus placebo in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. The full Results section reports 50 randomized and 47 treated, whereas the abstract calls 47 randomized. The treated cohort is the basis for the reported efficacy comparisons; both source formulations are retained here to prevent a sample-size transcription error.

The study was suspended for futility. PSA50 response was 41% versus 33%, with P = 0.44; progression-free survival, toxicity and quality of life were not significantly improved. Median overall survival was numerically shorter with ascorbate, 15.2 versus 29.5 months, but that difference was not statistically significant. This is evidence against routine use of that combination in that population, not proof that ascorbate causes shorter survival in general.

## Consequence for a continuation project

The positive and negative trials concern different cancers, regimens, exposure schedules and patient populations. Pooling their median survival values would be misleading. The useful lesson is to measure exposure and mechanism, identify the responsive context, and require a biological endpoint before claiming enhancement.

The NCI's current official statement says that high-dose vitamin C has not been FDA-approved as a cancer treatment. This is distinct from approvals for photosensitizer-based PDT. Oral supplementation is also not the intervention tested in these IV trials.
